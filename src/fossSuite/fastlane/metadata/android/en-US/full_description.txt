Clairix is a security app for a stock Android phone: no root, no custom ROM, no unlocked bootloader. It looks for what may be watching you, and gives you encrypted messages and calls through a relay you or someone you trust can run.

<b>What it checks</b>
<ul>
<li><b>Hidden cameras.</b> Point the phone around a room and it looks for the glint a lens throws back under the torch, and for infrared lights through the front camera. Nothing is saved unless you keep a photo of a finding.</li>
<li><b>Trackers.</b> Listens for AirTags, Tiles and similar Bluetooth tags, and says one is following you only once it has been heard at places far apart that your own position shows you travelled between.</li>
<li><b>Stalkerware.</b> Every day and after each install, checks apps against a public list of known stalkerware and weighs what each can do: permissions actually granted, access to your screen, notifications or keyboard, a hidden icon, and whether it is your default SMS, phone or home app. It says when someone else manages the phone, and reports a pairing or an install after an unlock while you slept. It can ask the phone's secure hardware how it booted, and read a bug report you share to show which apps used the microphone, camera and location.</li>
<li><b>The phone itself.</b> Certificates, proxies, USB debugging, patch level, VPN leaks, 2G, and a checklist of the accounts and sharing that let someone else see this phone.</li>
<li><b>Fake Wi-Fi and cell towers.</b> Warns about hotspots set up to impersonate networks your phone trusts, and about towers that break the rules for the places you go.</li>
<li><b>GPS.</b> Checks whether satellite signals look spoofed or jammed.</li>
</ul>

<b>If someone may be watching you</b>
Removing a stalkerware app can alert whoever installed it. Before Clairix opens an app's settings it says so, offers to save evidence first and points to a helpline. The evidence report is dated and signed on the phone. Discreet mode keeps notifications and the lock screen neutral, and a duress passcode wipes Clairix and leaves a decoy.

<b>Messages and calls</b>
Messages, photos, voice notes and calls are end-to-end encrypted on the phone (the Olm double ratchet, from the audited vodozemac library) and sent as sealed envelopes the relay cannot open and is not told the sender of. Groups, disappearing messages, blocking and message requests, an SOS to your groups with your position, a duress phrase, a fake call and a recovery file for a lost phone are included. Messaging needs a Clairix number: an invite from someone already on Clairix, or a relay you run yourself. The project relay runs on Cloudflare; anyone can run their own, and relays reach each other.

<b>Offline navigation</b>
Turn-by-turn directions from signed offline maps, covering the United States only for now, with the licence-plate cameras mapped in OpenStreetMap shown and routed around.

<b>What to know first</b>
<ul>
<li>This is the foss edition: the whole app, including the Card Vault with NFC card emulation and ClairixCoin, a non-monetary token passed between members of the same relay, built without Google Play services or Firebase.</li>
<li>Detection is best-effort. No app finds every camera, tracker or piece of spyware, and an app cannot see past a compromised operating system.</li>
<li>Positions come from Android's own location service, or from the satellite receiver alone with Satellite-only positioning on.</li>
<li>Besides the relay, calls may pass through Cloudflare's TURN servers, and with Share traffic data on, the relay asks HERE and TomTom for incidents near your route.</li>
<li>There are no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting libraries, and nothing reports back to the project.</li>
<li>Requires Android 12 or newer. Free software under the GNU GPL version 3.</li>
</ul>
