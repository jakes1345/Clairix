**Drive.** Offline turn-by-turn navigation on OpenStreetMap data, United States only for now (the 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico), with privacy-preserving live traffic: anonymous speed samples per road segment, never your GPS track, and nothing for roughly the first kilometre (a distance that varies per trip) or the final approach of a trip. Routing works offline; the background map shows only areas you viewed while online. Optional engine data from a Bluetooth OBD-II adapter, and with an RTL-SDR stick a channel scanner and the aircraft around you; the scanner records only when you turn recording on, and keeps recordings encrypted on the phone. The stick runs on the phone's battery: below 15 % with no charger, drive mode stops it and the adapter until the phone charges. Navigation takes a position every second during a route; below 15 % with no charger, its live traffic, speed reports and check for a faster route slow to every 5 minutes.

**NFC.** Read and identify NFC tags and cards, and flag ones that look like clones or emulators.

**Clean link.** Share a link to Clairix, or select it in any app and choose Clean link, and it comes back without what is there to follow whoever taps it: campaign tags (utm_ and the like), ad and mail click identifiers (fbclid, gclid, msclkid, mc_eid and others), and the share ids that YouTube, Spotify, Instagram, X, TikTok and others add. A link through Google's, Facebook's, Instagram's or YouTube's redirect page is unwrapped to the address it carries. It is done on the phone from Clairix's own list of rules, without opening the link or asking any server; then Copy, Open or Share. While Discreet mode is on, Clean link is not offered in other apps.

**Check a bug report.** Android's bug report (Developer options, Take bug report) includes the system's own records of which apps recently used the microphone, the camera and location. Share the report to Clairix and choose Check a bug report: it reads the zip on the phone, lists each app with the last time the report gives, and says which parts of the report it could not read, since the format changes between Android versions. Nothing is uploaded and no copy is kept. A report covers only what the phone still remembered, often a few days, and an app listed may have a good reason to be there. The screen reminds you to switch Developer options off again afterwards, which Phone check counts as a risk while they are on. While Discreet mode is on, it is not offered in the share sheet.

**Timeline and evidence reports.** Every finding is kept in a timeline on your phone, and the Timeline says how long before old entries are deleted. Export a human-readable report or a machine-readable JSON file for the period you pick, and share it with whoever you choose. It gives every time in UTC and in your phone's local time, the Clairix version, your phone's model, Android version and security patch, your retention and tracker settings, a summary of each device that travelled with you, device details and positions, the apps with spy-level access (who installed each and when, what it can reach, how much data it sent and received over the last week, its signing certificate), the phone's integrity checks, unlocks during your sleep window, the trust store and the hidden-camera sweep, with any photo you kept going along as a picture. Each report ends with a SHA-256 of its text and a signature by a key Clairix keeps in your phone's Android Keystore, so whoever receives it can check it was not edited (see Support). On a phone that supports it, a signing key made by this version also carries the Keystore's attestation certificates, which go in the report; Clairix does not check them against Google's attestation roots, which needs the internet, but whoever receives the report can. Every timeline entry also stores the SHA-256 of the entry written before it, and the report carries the newest one and whether the chain is whole, so an entry changed, removed or moved after the export can be found by comparing with a later copy. This does not prove when an entry was written: the times are the phone's clock, and someone in control of the phone could rewrite the whole timeline before an export. Entries removed by the retention are recorded as such, and a timeline kept from before this version is chained once, oldest first, without losing anything. Before it shares, Clairix warns you that the report holds your own location history.

**A tour of the app.** After setup, a tour walks through every place in the app and what it does, with a button to open each one. It is built from the app's own lists, so nothing in Clairix goes untoured, and it can be taken again from Settings.
